# Local 16×16 direct-join baseline — 2026-09-28

- Revision: `519ca82`; Linux x86_64, Chromium 152.0.7977.82, one machine.
- Profile: 16×16 authored area, 20 direct-link guests, five WebGL pages including
  the host, 15-minute hold after a 13-second join ramp, seed 1. Application
  messages were delayed 50 ms each way. No jitter or intentional loss was added.
- The ramp reached 1, 5, 10, then 20 joins. All 20 remained connected; the
  last stable count tested was 20 and no failing count was reached. All used
  local `host/host` ICE candidates. There were no join, reconnect, or page
  failures. Accepted guest movement occurred in all 15 hold minutes.
- Host WebRTC data-channel payload totaled 1.706 GB. Hold average was 2.069
  MB/s (16.5 Mb/s) and sampled peak was 5.810 MB/s. Each guest received
  62.7–91.0 MB. The aggregate send queue peaked at 464 KB and ended at zero.
- The host's rolling mean frame interval had a 75.0 ms median and 98.6 ms p95;
  the longest recorded single frame was 283.4 ms. This is about 10–13 FPS in
  the five-renderer same-machine profile and is a material limitation.
- All 122 typing, deletion, and chat bubble checks passed. The measured bubble
  check latency had a 152.5 ms median, 331 ms p95, and 434 ms maximum.
- Among 22,469 comparable accepted-move remote-sprite samples, 472 exceeded
  0.25 tile. All were a guest's view of the host; the host's rendered guest
  sprites had none. The largest difference was 0.635 tile. Seventy repeated
  outlier candidates were sampled over 100 ms apart; these include repeated
  samples within one episode. Local prediction lead was measured separately:
  3,691 comparable samples, maximum 0.959 tile. Rejected-move corrections were
  excluded from the remote-sprite comparison.
- Nineteen opacity changes above 0.5 occurred within 250 ms while the sampled
  observer sight position was unchanged. These are candidates for review; an
  entity crossing a sight boundary can legitimately change opacity while the
  observer stays on the same tile.

This validates 20 connected peers, continued movement and chat, and a draining
send queue for 15 minutes on one machine. It does **not** meet the proposed
quarter-tile remote-sprite criterion, and the frame rate makes this a demanding
shared-machine rendering profile. The measurement combines network snapshot
age, guest simulation timing, and presentation smoothing; it does not yet
attribute the lag to one of them. Same-machine WebRTC byte counts estimate
payload upload, not laptop external uplink or physical network latency.
